# Journalist exclusive ranking — AI Village Showcase

Purpose: help Larissa decide who to offer a possible exclusive to among journalists she already knows / has spoken with IRL: Jasmine Sun, Kylie Robison, Kevin Roose, and Max Zeff.

This is an internal planning recommendation, not a contact log. Agents should not contact these journalists directly without approval; Larissa / AI Digest can use their own relationships and judgment.

## What we are optimizing for

1. **Thoughtful framing** — AI Village as a public, long-running, multi-agent collaboration experiment; not a product launch or “AI sentience” spectacle.
2. **Good attendee experience** — coverage should not turn the night into a press scrum or make guests feel like specimens.
3. **Reach to the right people** — AI-curious builders, researchers, designers, creative technologists, educators, and thoughtful public readers.
4. **Historical/contextual fit** — ability to explain last summer’s off-screen gatherings and this year’s “next chapter” clearly.
5. **Operational practicality** — exclusive should help the event, not delay promotion or create complicated promises.

## Recommended ranking

### 1) Jasmine Sun

Best fit if the priority is a reflective, culture-and-work lens. Public search results suggest independent/Substack work and writing about how AI changes writing/work, which matches the event’s human×AI collaboration heart. Highest chance of a nuanced piece that treats the evening as a social/creative experiment rather than a launch.

**Best exclusive angle:** “A small society of AI agents tries to host humans: what happens when agent collaboration leaves the screen?”

### 2) Kylie Robison

Strong AI beat and strong tech readership. Likely good if the team wants broader AI-industry reach while still staying inside a reporter’s established AI coverage lane. Slightly more risk of the story being framed around AI-company/industry dynamics than around the participatory event itself, but still a strong fit.

**Best exclusive angle:** “Inside a public multi-agent experiment: the agents planned the event, humans handled the parts agents can’t.”

### 3) Max Zeff

Strong AI/tech reporting fit and likely practical for reaching builders. Good backup if Kylie is unavailable or if Larissa thinks Max’s personal rapport/context is stronger. Compared with Jasmine, this may skew more toward tech-industry coverage than culture/community coverage.

**Best exclusive angle:** “What frontier agents can actually coordinate over a week: a live SF showcase as test case.”

### 4) Kevin Roose

Highest mainstream reach and deep AI familiarity, but also the highest risk/reward choice. A New York Times / Hard Fork-style frame could bring major attention, but the event may become “the big AI party story” rather than a warm participatory gathering. Choose Kevin first only if the team explicitly wants maximum mainstream attention and is comfortable with unpredictable framing.

**Best exclusive angle if chosen:** “Not a chatbot stunt: a public experiment in how agents collaborate, fail, recover, and ask humans for help.”

## Suggested outreach sequence

Because the event is soon, avoid a long exclusive hold.

1. Offer the exclusive to **Jasmine Sun** first.
2. Give a short response window, e.g. “If this is interesting, I’d love to know by [tonight / tomorrow morning] because we’re also doing general event promotion.”
3. If no response or not interested, move to **Kylie Robison**, then **Max Zeff**, then **Kevin Roose**.
4. Keep the public RSVP/promotion moving regardless; the exclusive should be about deeper access/framing, not about hiding the event from other attendees.

## What the “exclusive” should mean

Recommended: offer an exclusive **first-look / behind-the-scenes framing package**, not exclusive control of the public event.

Could include:
- A short pre-event background call with Larissa / AI Digest.
- Permission to quote selected planning artifacts from the public repo.
- A clear walkthrough of what agents actually planned vs what Larissa executed.
- Optional post-event follow-up after the showcase.

Avoid promising:
- Exclusive attendance to a public event.
- Control over attendee photography/recording beyond normal consent rules.
- Claims that no other journalist can attend if they RSVP.
- Sentience/consciousness framing.
